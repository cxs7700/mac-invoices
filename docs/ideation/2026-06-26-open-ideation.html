Ideation: Mac Invoices — where to take it next

Date 2026-06-26  ·  Topic open-ideation  ·  Focus open-ended (surprise-me, whole app)  ·  Mode repo-grounded

24 candidate ideas generated across six frames, critiqued against the project's binding constraints, narrowed to 7 survivors. The throughline: the app already holds an unusual set of assets — a no-login contractor portal no competitor has, an append-only event ledger, a free scheduled-job harness, and a per-property model. The strongest ideas are recombinations of what's already built, not new infrastructure.

Grounding Context

The product. A single landlord's rental-property invoice manager plus a no-login contractor submission portal. React+Vite SPA, Fastify+Prisma+Postgres API, shared Zod schemas, Vercel (not yet deployed), EN/ZH.

Leverage points (where new features are cheap):

  • The append-only InvoiceEvent ledger — full JSON detail, already feeds the in-app feed and the email digest. Audit / timeline / anomaly work rides on it with no schema change.
  • The storage seam (owner-gated Blob, signed upload tokens) and the contractor token pattern (no-login `inv_<lookupId>_<secret>`).
  • The digest cron (free Vercel/GitHub-Actions schedule, reads the ledger, sends Resend email) — extra reminders/jobs are "one more query + template."
  • The Properties model with per-property spend rollup, and shared Zod schemas (a new field propagates validation to API + web form automatically).

Verified gaps: no payments, no OCR, no tax/Schedule-E output, no due-date alerts, no recurring invoices, thin contractor identity (name + unvalidated free-text contact).

Binding constraints (ideas were filtered against these): cost-aversion (lead with the $0 path; SMS rejected; paid OCR deferred) · single-owner model (extend via lightweight token-scoped non-User entities, never a second login) · Postgres is the source of truth (integrations are one-way export) · reuse the seams · pre-launch posture.

Market position: No competitor (Stessa, Baselane, REI Hub, Landlord Studio, TinyLord) has a no-login contractor portal; Buildium/AppFolio require contractor accounts at $50–200+/mo. The whitespace is "contractor intake + Schedule E + per-property reporting at $0–15/mo." Table-stakes gaps every competitor fills: Schedule E categorization, receipt OCR, mileage, recurring expenses, per-property P&L.

Decomposition skipped — surprise-me mode (no fixed subject to decompose; cross-frame synthesis served the coverage role instead).

Ranked Ideas

Jump to:
  1. Schedule E tax layer — categorize, see YTD, export the packet · tax / reporting
  2. Three-way match — turn “Approve” into a verification gate · controls

1Schedule E tax layer — categorize, see YTD, export the packet Top pick

Description
Derive the 15 IRS Schedule E lines from the existing 6-value category enum (a code-side mapping table — no new taxonomy the landlord has to learn), add a permanent dashboard tile showing YTD deductions by Schedule E line, per property, and a one-click year-end CSV/PDF packet per property (invoices + photos + their event timeline). Its sharpest sub-feature: a $0 rules-prompt at the highest-audit-risk decision — when a REPAIRS invoice exceeds the IRS de-minimis safe-harbor amount, ask "repair (deduct now) or improvement (depreciate)?" and record the answer in the ledger.
Basis
direct The InvoiceCategory enum and the Property totalSpend rollup (sum amount, exclude REJECTED/CANCELLED, scope by userId) already exist; APPROVE already requires a category + property, so categorization is captured at the right moment. external Schedule E categorization is the #1 table-stakes gap every competitor fills as reports; repairs-vs-improvements is the highest-audit-risk judgment "un-automated by all" — and the named market whitespace is exactly "Schedule E + per-property reporting at $0–15/mo."
Rationale
This is simultaneously the biggest market gap and one of the cheapest builds, because the expensive parts (the properties model, the rollup, the ledger, category-at-approval) already shipped. It converts "a pile of invoices" into the one artifact a landlord actually needs once a year, and the repairs-vs-improvements nudge is a judgment feature competitors can't copy by adding a column.
Downsides
The category→Schedule-E mapping is opinionated and the repairs-vs-improvements line is a real tax judgment — surface it as guidance, not advice, with a clear "confirm with your accountant" stance. A $0 HTML-to-PDF render needs verifying on Vercel (else CSV-only first).
Confidence High (85%)Complexity MediumBasis direct + external

2Three-way match — turn “Approve” into a verification gate

Description
Borrow accounts-payable three-way matching and reframe approval from a status flip into a reconciliation check: before a landlord can APPROVE, the review card triangulates the typed amount, the attached photo(s), and the category/property, and surfaces mismatches (a PARTS photo but a "cleaning" category; an amount with no receipt photo). The richer variant adds a token-scoped WorkOrder (agreed scope + price, created before the job) so a submitted amount over the agreed price raises a "match exception." Every approval writes its rationale to the ledger.
Basis
external AP two/three-way matching (PO ↔ invoice ↔ receipt must agree before money moves — Stampli, Tipalti) — grounding flags this triangle as built by no property-management tool. direct All three legs already exist: InvoiceImage with type enum, the typed amount, and the APPROVE-requires-category+property guard; the ledger already accepts arbitrary detail JSON for the rationale.
Rationale
A landlord paying real cash out of pocket fears overbilling and scope-creep most. This imports a battle-tested financial control onto data the app already collects, and the no-login portal plus matching is whitespace nobody occupies — it makes the contractor differentiator defensible.
Downsides
The lean version (validate the three present legs) is cheap; the WorkOrder variant adds a new entity + a second contractor link and is a bigger commitment — stage it. Risk of over-gating a trusting landlord/contractor relationship; keep exceptions advisory, not hard blocks, except for the missing-photo case.
Confidence High (80%) leanComplexity MediumBasis external + direct

3Recurring invoices on the cron you already run

Description
Add a RecurringTemplate (vendor, amount-or-blank, category, property, cadence, nextRunAt). The existing digest cron that runs every ~15 min also checks for due templates and auto-creates a draft PENDING invoice (or a "bill due" reminder when the amount varies, like a water bill). The landlord just reviews and approves — no re-typing utilities, pest control, or inspection vendors every cycle. Each materialization is a ledger event, so the recurring chain stays auditable.
Basis
direct The GitHub-Actions/Vercel cron + CRON_SECRET endpoint already exists and reads the ledger; shared Zod schemas propagate a new entity to API + web automatically. external Recurring expenses are a table-stakes gap every competitor fills; cron-materialized recurring transactions are standard in QuickBooks/YNAB.
Rationale
Predictable monthly/quarterly bills are the bulk of a landlord's expenses and the most annoying to re-enter. This makes the next 50 invoices cheaper to create, the marginal infra cost is zero (it piggybacks a job already running), and drafts-not-auto-approvals keeps the human in the loop.
Downsides
Templates drift from reality (a vendor changes price); blank-amount "reminder only" mode handles variable bills but adds a second path. Needs idempotency so a cron retry doesn't double-create — the digest's at-least-once stamping pattern is the model to copy.
Confidence High (82%)Complexity MediumBasis direct + external

4Due-date & overdue reminders in the digest quick win

Description
The dueDate field is captured and displayed but never acted on. Add one query to the digest flush: APPROVED invoices with dueDate within N days and paidDate == null, grouped per landlord, folded into the existing HTML email as a "Due soon / Overdue" section (escalated tone when past due). One SELECT and one template block — no new channel, no new job.
Basis
direct digest.ts already queries the ledger, groups by landlord, sends via Resend, and stamps idempotently; Invoice already carries dueDate + paidDate. Grounding: "due-date reminders = one more query + template." external AP aging-bucket reminders (Bill.com).
Rationale
Highest value-to-effort ratio in the set: a captured-but-unused field activated into infrastructure that already runs. A landlord paying contractors out of pocket cares about cash-flow timing; a missed due date is a real late fee or an annoyed contractor.
Downsides
Reminder cadence/dedup needs care (don't re-nag every 15 min — stamp "reminded" or bucket by day). Minor.
Confidence High (88%)Complexity LowBasis direct

5History auto-fill — the $0 answer to OCR

Description
Instead of parsing a photo (paid vision, repeatedly deferred), parse the app's own history. When a vendorName (or contractor) matches a prior invoice, pre-fill the most-recent category, property, and typical amount as editable defaults. Complement it on the capture side: give the contractor a guided structured form (line items, category hint) so the person holding the receipt does the structuring — for free, more accurately than OCR on a crumpled receipt.
Basis
reasoned OCR's value is "don't retype." For a repeat vendor, history gives the same payoff (category, property, vendor) deterministically and for free — and a photo can't supply category/property anyway. OCR only adds value for first-seen vendors, so history-fill captures most of the benefit at none of the cost — and leads with the $0 path exactly as cost-aversion demands. direct The invoice list already does vendor/description search, so the lookup is cheap. external "Receipt OCR / auto-fill" is a named table-stakes gap.
Rationale
Attacks the most-cited friction (hand-typing) and the most-requested table-stake (auto-fill) while explicitly sidestepping the paid-OCR cost the project keeps deferring. For a landlord with recurring vendors, it removes most of the typing OCR would.
Downsides
Only helps repeat vendors (first-seen still typed) — but those are the painful, repetitive ones. Stale defaults if a vendor's pricing shifts; defaults stay editable so the cost is low.
Confidence Med-High (75%)Complexity MediumBasis reasoned + direct

6Duplicate & anomaly detection — “Drift,” generalized

Description
The Sheets export already computes a Drifted state (local data changed after sync) — a diff muscle. Generalize it into a free in-app anomaly pass over the ledger: the same vendor billing twice for a near-identical amount in a short window (duplicate-invoice catch), an invoice approved above a property's running budget, or a contractor amount that's an outlier versus their own history. Surface as a non-blocking flag in the feed — SQL + thresholds, no paid ML.
Basis
direct The Drift diff-detection and the per-property spend rollup already exist; grounding: "anomaly-detection rides on the ledger with no schema change." external Duplicate-invoice detection is a core feature AP automation (Tipalti, Stampli) sells to finance teams; Expensify flags expense outliers.
Rationale
Duplicate-payment and overbilling protection is the most valuable thing AP automation sells, and here it's recombination, not new construction — the drift pattern and the ledger are both built. Pairs naturally with #2 as the "soft" detection beneath the "hard" approval gate.
Downsides
Threshold tuning to avoid false-positive fatigue; keep flags advisory and dismissible. Value scales with invoice volume — thinner for a very small portfolio.
Confidence Med (68%)Complexity MediumBasis direct + external

7Close the contractor loop — notify on approve/reject

Description
The contractor's status page already exists (their token returns submission status + rejection reason), but they're never told when something changes — the digest emails only the landlord, because the contractor contact field is unvalidated free-text. Add an optional validated email on Contractor; when present, fire a Resend email on approve/reject (and optionally paid) via the same seam the digest uses. Falls back silently to no-notify when there's no email. Never SMS.
Basis
direct The contractor read-back endpoint already exposes status + rejectionReason; the Resend sendEmail seam is built; grounding records that contractor notifications were deferred "until contact becomes a validated channel," and SMS is explicitly rejected. external Magic-link customer/vendor status + email (Housecall Pro, Light.inc).
Rationale
The no-login portal is the app's named differentiator, yet the contractor's experience dead-ends at submission. Closing the loop deepens the one thing no competitor has, leads with the $0 email path, and respects the documented "validated channel first" gate instead of overriding it.
Downsides
Requires the contractor to provide a real email (many give a phone) — so adoption is partial; the silent fallback keeps it harmless. Adds an outbound email surface to verify against spam/deliverability before launch.
Confidence Med (70%)Complexity Low-MedBasis direct + external
